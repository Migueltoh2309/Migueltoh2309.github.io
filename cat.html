Quadruped Cat Robot

A low-cost four-legged robot inspired by feline locomotion. Each leg has two degrees of freedom (hip and knee), and the motion is computed with floating-base inverse kinematics: given the desired position and orientation of the body and the position of each foot on the ground, the model returns the angles of the eight servos.

The kinematics were first developed and validated in MATLAB, moving the body up and down, forward and back and pitching it while the feet stay fixed on the ground (animation above, regenerated from the same equations). The same model was then ported to C++ on an ESP32 that drives the eight servos, with standing, sitting, lying, stretching and trot-gait walking commanded over Bluetooth.

  • Floating-Base Inverse Kinematics for Legged Robots
  • Gait Generation and Posture Control
  • Embedded Implementation on ESP32 with Bluetooth Control
