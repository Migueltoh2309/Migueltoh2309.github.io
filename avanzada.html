Vision-Based Intuitive Arm Motion Transmission for Tele-Surgery Using the Sawyer Robot

The global shortage of specialized surgeons poses a major challenge to providing equitable surgical care, especially in underserved regions where geographic and professional barriers limit access. This study presents an intuitive telesurgery system based on computer vision, using a standard webcam to allow surgeons to perform remote operations without bulky or expensive equipment. A motion transmission system was also developed for the Sawyer robot to accurately replicate human hand trajectories. Experiments focused on basic surgical movements, yielding mean absolute errors of 2.36 mm (horizontal), 2.49 mm (vertical), and 3.93 mm (circular), all within the 2.2 mm to 5.5 mm operational range reported in previous studies. Compared to existing high-cost systems, the proposed solution is more accessible and affordable, making telesurgery more viable in low-resource settings. The results highlight the system’s potential to shorten training times and improve movement accuracy, supporting the adoption of robot-assisted telesurgery in underserved areas.
